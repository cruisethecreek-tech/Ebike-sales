E-bikes turn steep hills into molehills and let you explore further with less strain. Whether you're commuting, running errands or out for a weekend spin, this guide covers what you need to ride legally, safely and smoothly in Mahoning County.

E-Bike Classes & Local Rules

Ohio law defines an e-bike as a bike with working pedals and a motor under 750 watts, sorted into three classes. Mill Creek MetroParks adds its own rules for the Bikeway.

Class 1 · Pedal-assist, 20 mph
Bikeway OK

The motor helps only while you pedal and cuts out at 20 mph.

Class 2 · Throttle, 20 mph
Bikeway OK

Has a throttle that can move the bike without pedaling, up to 20 mph.

Class 3 · Pedal-assist, 28 mph
Not on Bikeway

Faster pedal-assist with a speedometer. Not allowed on the MetroParks Bikeway. Every Class 3 rider and passenger must wear a helmet, and riders under 16 may not operate one.

15 mph on the Bikeway

The 15 mph limit applies to every rider on the MetroParks Bikeway, whether you're on a throttle, pedal assist or pure leg power. The "no motorized vehicles" signs are aimed at gas-powered machines; legal Class 1 and 2 e-bikes follow the same rules as regular bikes.

Know the rules

  • No license, registration or insurance needed for a legal e-bike in Ohio.
  • Helmets are strongly recommended for everyone and required on Class 3.
  • Foot trails are for walkers only. In the park, bikes belong on park drives, paved hike & bike trails and trails marked for bikes.
  • Sidewalks: motor off. Ohio only allows an e-bike on a sidewalk with the motor not engaged.
  • Hours: the Bikeway is open 5 am to 10 pm. The rest of the park closes half an hour after sunset.
  • After dark: use a white front light and a red rear light.
  • On the road you have the same rights and duties as any cyclist: ride with traffic and obey signs and signals.

Rule reminder: check the trail signs, yield to people on foot and give an audible signal before you pass.

Best Rides & Routes

MetroParks Bikeway
Easy
11 miles · paved · gentle grades

A fully paved multi-use trail from Western Reserve Road in Canfield Township north to the Mahoning/Trumbull county line in Austintown Township. You'll pass MetroParks Farm, Sawmill Creek Preserve and the trestle-style Mahoning Avenue overpass, and ride a tunnel under the Ohio Turnpike. It's part of the Great Ohio Lake-to-River Greenway.

Mill Creek Park Hike & Bike Trails
Hilly
7+ miles · paved · Route 224 to Ford Nature Center

A chain of paved trails through the gorge, past the lakes and Lanterman's Mill:

  • East Golf Hike & Bike Trail: 1.5 mi, closed to cars, Route 224 to Shields Rd. Parking at both ends, restrooms and a drinking fountain.
  • East Newport Hike & Bike Trail: 1.75 mi bike lane on a one-way park road, with views of Lake Newport and the wetlands.
  • East Cohasset Hike & Bike Trail: 1.5 mi, closed to cars, above Lake Cohasset.
  • Old Mill Hike & Bike Trail: a quarter mile overlooking the gorge, connecting to Lanterman's Mill.
Connecting Greenways
Longer rides

The Bikeway runs straight into the 4.5-mile Niles Greenway at its north end. Warren is about 5 to 6 miles further by road, where you can pick up the Western Reserve Greenway.

Downtown Youngstown
On-street

Bike lanes on Fifth Avenue, Federal Street and Front Street link downtown with YSU and Wean Park.

Charging & Battery Safety

  • Look for UL 2849. It certifies the e-bike's whole electrical system (battery, motor and charger) against overheating and fire.
  • Use the charger made for your bike. Never charge a battery that is cracked, leaking or bulging.
  • Don't charge unattended, and don't block doorways or exits with your bike or battery.
  • Store batteries at room temperature, out of the rain, ideally between 30% and 60% charge.
  • Recycle old batteries through a bike shop or Call2Recycle, never the trash.
Plan your range

There are no public e-bike charging outlets along the trails. Charge at home before you ride and plan your route around your battery.
