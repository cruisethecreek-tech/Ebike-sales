The job

What a tour day actually looks like

Guiding at Mill Creek Park is part ride leader, part host, part safety net. Here's the shape of a shift, so nobody applies expecting something else.

🔧
Before the guests arrive
Pull the bikes for the booking, check tires, brakes, and battery charge, and stage helmets. Anything that isn't trail-ready gets flagged before it goes out, not after.
👋
Check-in & fitting
Greet the group, match riders to the right bike and seat height, confirm waivers, and run the safety and controls walkthrough. Guests arrive 15 minutes early for exactly this.
🚵
Leading the ride
Set a pace the slowest rider can hold, keep the group together at crossings and junctions, and know the park well enough to tell people what they're looking at. Mixed-ability groups are the norm.
🧰
When something goes sideways
Flat tire, a nervous first-timer, weather rolling in, a battery lower than it should be. You handle it calmly on the trail and call it in when it's beyond a trailside fix.
🔋
After the ride
Bikes back, batteries on charge, quick wipe-down and damage check, helmets sorted. The next group's experience is decided by how the last shift closed out.
📣
The part that isn't riding
Answering questions about the bikes, pointing people to rentals or the shop, and being the face of Cruise the Creek in the park. A lot of our bookings start with a good conversation on the trail.
The fit

What we look for

None of this is a checklist you have to clear perfectly. If you're strong on the people side, we can teach the rest.

✓
You're good with people first
Half this job is making a nervous first-time rider feel safe. Patience beats bike knowledge every time.
✓
You can ride and talk at once
Confident enough on an e-bike to lead a group, watch your riders, and narrate — not white-knuckling the bars.
✓
You know the park, or you'll learn it fast
Mill Creek is 4,400 acres. Local knowledge is a real advantage, but we'll ride the routes with you until they're second nature.
✓
You're reliable on a weekend
Tours run when people are off work. If Saturday mornings are sacred, this probably isn't the right fit — and that's fine, say so.
✓
You keep a level head
Weather turns, chains drop, someone oversells their fitness. We want the person who solves it without making guests feel it.
✓
You'd be proud of the fleet
Clean bikes, charged batteries, helmets that don't smell. The details are the job.
No experience guiding? Apply anyway.
Some of the best people on a trail have never worked a tour in their life. Tell us what you have done — coaching, teaching, serving, retail, camp counseling, group rides with friends — and we'll read it.
